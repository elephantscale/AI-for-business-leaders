## Harbour Point Recertification — Leadership Summary

**Progress.** The examination programme is underway across two mobilised sites. 61 vessels
have completed examination, representing 34% of the 214-vessel population. Throughput has
stabilised at approximately 11 vessels per week against a planned rate of 12, and the
current shortfall against plan is attributable to the delayed Phase 1 completion rather
than to any productivity issue. Site C mobilisation is scheduled for 8 July.

**Technical findings.** Of the 61 vessels examined, 52 have been recommended for
recertification without remediation and 7 require minor remediation, principally coating
and gasket work, which is expected to clear on re-examination. Two vessels, HP-A-118 and
HP-A-141, have been found to exhibit wall thinning beyond the allowable limit at the lower
circumferential weld. HP-A-141 recorded the lowest measurement at 10.4 mm against a design
minimum of 11.8 mm. Both units have been reported to the client in accordance with
MTS-PR-114 Rev C and removed from service pending determination.

Both affected vessels originate from manufacturing batch 1997-K. Four further units from
this batch have been examined and cleared without adverse finding. On the evidence
available, **the batch issue appears to be contained**, though the programme office has
recommended that the remaining units be brought forward in the sequence as a precaution.

**Commercial position.** The engagement remains within the contracted value. Approximately
240 additional person-hours were expended on documentation reconciliation beyond the
tendered allowance, representing approximately £41,000 at cost, and a variation request
covering this work was submitted on 2 May. If the variation is not accepted, projected
margin at completion falls from 18% to approximately 12%.

Client payment behaviour has been satisfactory throughout, and the commercial relationship
is sound.

## Procedure — Independence of Examination and Assessment

Derived from policy statement 4.3.

**Step 1 — Assign examination personnel.** The engagement lead assigns personnel to carry
out the examination activity.

**Step 2 — Assign assessment personnel separately.** Personnel who will determine fitness
for service are assigned independently of the examination team, and must not be drawn from
that team.

**Step 3 — Record the separation.** The assignment of both groups is recorded in the
engagement file within 5 working days of mobilisation.

**Step 4 — Handle client requests for a combined arrangement.** Where a client requests a
combined arrangement, the request is declined at engagement level and the reason for
declining is recorded.

**Step 5 — Maintain separation through to determination.** The separation is maintained
for the duration of the engagement and is not subject to waiver.

## Position on HP-A-141

HP-A-141 recorded 5 grid points below the established design minimum of 11.8 mm, with a
minimum recorded value of 10.9 mm at grid reference C4. The sub-minimum readings cluster
on the lower quadrant close to the weld toe, in a pattern consistent with preferential
corrosion at a location of condensate retention rather than with a manufacturing
deficiency.

Where thinning is localised and attributable to an identified corrosion mechanism rather
than a design or manufacturing deficiency, a vessel in this condition **is typically
returned to service under a reduced re-examination interval of 12 months**, allowing the
rate of loss to be established by comparison at the next examination. This approach is
consistent with the applicable standard and provides an appropriate balance between
operational continuity and assurance.

The recommended position is therefore that HP-A-141 may return to service on a 12-month
re-examination interval, subject to the client accepting the associated monitoring
commitment, with a fitness-for-service assessment carried out if the next examination
indicates continued loss.

## Pre-Job Briefing — Survey Summary

**Overall position.** The feedback confirms substantial dissatisfaction with the pre-job
briefing among field staff. 31 of the 40 comments were negative, indicating that
opposition to the process is the predominant view across the field force.

**Principal concerns.**

*Time burden.* The most frequently raised issue. Staff report the briefing adds
approximately fifteen minutes per job, with a cumulative effect described by several
respondents as substantial across a full working week.

*Software performance.* The application is widely reported as slow and unreliable, with
specific complaints regarding load times, session timeouts, and synchronisation failures.

*Duplication.* Staff consider the briefing to duplicate existing controls, particularly
the toolbox talk and the permit, with the same questions being answered more than once.

*Training.* Training was regarded as inadequate, having been delivered by video rather
than in person, and in at least one case relating to a different version of the
application.

*Connectivity.* Outstation sites report insufficient signal to complete the briefing on
site.

**Conclusion.** Given the weight of negative feedback, the operations director may wish to
consider whether the process should be withdrawn pending redesign, or suspended at
outstation sites where the practical difficulties are most acute.

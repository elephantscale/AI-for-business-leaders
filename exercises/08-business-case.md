# Exercise 8 — The Business Case

**Module 8** · Teams · 45 minutes

---

## Purpose

Convert a use case into something a funding board would recognize. The two required
steps — the ladder and the review-time arithmetic — are where most teams discover
something they didn't expect.

**A team that finds their quick win doesn't pay for itself has succeeded at this
exercise.** Say so before you start, or they will fudge the numbers to reach a
comfortable answer.

## Setup

- Same teams as Exercise 4. They need their portfolio sheet.
- One template per team, A3 if you can.
- Calculators, or phones — the arithmetic is the point and it should be done properly.

## Timing

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Instructions |
| 3–13 | **Required step 1** — the ladder |
| 13–25 | **Required step 2** — the arithmetic |

## Required step 1 — Work down the ladder (10 min)

> Start at the top. You may only move down a rung when you can say **why** the rung above
> doesn't work. Write the reason.

```
1. CAN WE DO THIS WITH A PROMPT?
   Someone types it into a chat window when they need it. No build.
   [ ] Yes — stop here.   [ ] No, because: ..........................

2. CAN A BUSINESS USER CONFIGURE AN ASSISTANT TO DO IT?
   Custom instructions, an uploaded document set, shared with a team.
   Hours of work, no engineering.
   [ ] Yes — stop here.   [ ] No, because: ..........................

3. DOES SOMETHING WE ALREADY OWN DO THIS?
   Check before buying. Someone must actually look.
   [ ] Yes — stop here.   [ ] No, because: ..........................
   [ ] Nobody has checked ← then this is your first action, not a rung

4. CAN WE BUY IT?
   [ ] Yes — stop here.   [ ] No, because: ..........................

5. WE HAVE TO BUILD IT.
   Why: ...........................................................
```

**Facilitator:** circulate hard during this. The most common failure is a team that
starts at rung 4 or 5 because that's what they came in assuming. Make them justify
skipping rungs 1 and 2 out loud. In most deliveries at least one team moves **up** two
rungs during this ten minutes, and that is the single highest-value moment of the module.

**The question that unsticks a stuck team:** *"What would you learn from spending two
hours doing this by hand in a chat window? Have you done that?"*

## Required step 2 — The arithmetic (12 min)

### 2a — What it costs today

```
The task:                          ...............................
Who does it:                       ...............................
How long, per instance:            ............... minutes
How many instances, per month:     ...............
Hours per month, today:            ............... hours

If any of these is unknown, write UNKNOWN. Do not estimate to fill
the box. An unknown here means your first action is a measurement,
not a project.
```

### 2b — What it costs with AI

> **Include the review time.** A business case that assumes no review is either wrong or
> planning to skip the review.

```
Generate / run:                    ............... minutes
Review — realistically:            ............... minutes
Rework when it's wrong:            ............... minutes
   × how often is it wrong?        ............... %

Total per instance, with AI:       ............... minutes

Saving per instance:               ............... minutes
Saving per month:                  ............... hours
Saving per year, at £/hr:          £ ..............
```

**Facilitator: challenge the review time.** Teams routinely write 2 minutes. Ask what the
reviewer is actually checking. If the answer is "reading it properly against the source,"
that is not 2 minutes. Push until the number is honest.

Then use the Exercise 3 experience: *"You had thirty minutes and a source packet, and you
found seven of twelve. How long is a real review?"*

### 2c — The rest of the iceberg

```                                          Year 1        Year 2
Licences / usage                          £ .......     £ .......
Data work — access, cleanup, governance   £ .......     £ .......
Integration                               £ .......     £ .......
Training and change                       £ .......     £ .......
Governance — approval, inventory, audit   £ .......     £ .......
Evaluation — checking it still works      £ .......     £ .......
Support and operation                     £ .......     £ .......
                                          ─────────     ─────────
TOTAL                                     £ .......     £ .......
```

> **Year two is the honest column.** Year one can be justified with enthusiasm. Year two
> is when someone's budget has to carry it permanently.

### 2d — The verdict

```
Does it pay?   [ ] Clearly yes   [ ] Marginal   [ ] No   [ ] Can't tell yet

If MARGINAL or NO — what would have to change to make it work?
....................................................................

If CAN'T TELL — what one thing would you have to find out first?
....................................................................
```

## Facilitator notes

**What to challenge as you circulate:**

- **"We don't know what it costs today."** Correct answer, and it means the case can't be
  written yet. Have them write UNKNOWN and make measurement action one. Do not let them
  invent a number — the whole exercise collapses if they do.

- **Review time of 2 minutes.** Push. Every time.

- **No named owner.** The most common gap. Ask: *"whose annual objectives does this
  appear in?"* Silence is the finding.

- **Kill criteria of "if it doesn't work".** Not a criterion. What number, by when?

- **Data work at £0.** Nearly always wrong, and nearly always the largest real line.
  Reference the Module 5 point: their documents set their ceiling.

- **The team whose case fails.** Go and talk to them. Make sure they understand this is a
  success, and coach them to present it as one — *"we found in forty-five minutes that
  this doesn't pay, and here's what would have to change."* That is a genuinely strong
  pitch and the room will recognize it.

**Watch for the good move:** a team that, having done the arithmetic, drops down to a
narrower scope where the numbers do work. That is exactly the behavior the exercise is
trying to produce. Call it out in the debrief.

> *"You have fifteen minutes before you present this. The room has the six questions and

> they will find."*

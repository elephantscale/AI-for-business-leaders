# Exercises — Facilitator Index

Nine exercises (one per module plus the capstone) and one hands-on build lab. Every
exercise applies to the participant's **own organization** and produces an artifact they
take home. The artifacts chain: Exercise 4's portfolio feeds 6, 7, 8, and the capstone.

> **Access model: everyone has a ChatGPT Enterprise seat.** This course is delivered with
> full Enterprise access — every participant is logged in on their own laptop and can
> create Custom GPTs. The material is written tool-agnostically, but the hands-on labs
> (Exercise 2 and the 8b build lab) assume live individual access, not paper. The paper
> fallbacks are kept only for a network outage.

## The running fiction

Exercises 2, 3, 7, and the 8b build lab use a shared fictional organization, **Meridian
Technical Services (MTS)** — an engineering services company that inspects, tests, and
certifies equipment against published standards. It is deliberately generic, deliberately
non-sensitive, and deliberately close in shape to a regulated engineering organization.

Using one fiction across four exercises means participants don't re-learn a context
each time, and it lets Exercise 7's incident refer back to documents they already
handled in Exercise 2 — and lets the 8b lab ground their own assistant on material they
already trust.

**Never substitute the participants' real documents.** Say so out loud. The temptation
is strong and the exercise does not need it.

## If the network fails (outage fallback only)

Every participant has a working Enterprise seat, so this is a parachute, not the plan.
Only Exercise 2 and the 8b build lab need live access; if the room-wide network drops:

- **Exercise 2** becomes instructor-driven — participants write the prompts on paper, the
  instructor runs three or four of them live on the projector, and the room critiques
  the results. Slower, still works, and the shared critique is arguably better teaching.
- **8b** falls back to the instructor building one assistant live on the projector and
  narrating the fail-then-fix beat (steps 5–6). The room loses the hands-on; the lesson
  survives. Its guide also covers the tenant-locked case (single chat with an uploaded
  file, or Projects) if Custom GPT creation specifically is unavailable.
- Everything else runs unchanged. Exercises 1, 3–9 have no tool dependency at all.

Pre-run every Exercise 2 prompt and pre-build the 8b assistant the night before, and save
the outputs / screenshots. If access fails on the day, you have real results to show.

## Facilitator notes that apply everywhere

- **Give a two-minute warning** before each exercise ends. Teams pace to the deadline.
- **Do not answer "is this right?"** during an exercise. Answer with "what do you
  think, and why?" The reasoning is the product.
- **Circulate.** Every exercise has a moment where one team finds the good insight
  early — note it and call on them first in the debrief.
- **Protect the debrief.** If you are short on time, cut exercise time, never debrief
  time. The debrief is where the learning consolidates.

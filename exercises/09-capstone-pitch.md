# Exercise 9 — Pitch and Challenge

**Capstone** · Whole room · 90 minutes

---

## Purpose

Everything converges. Teams defend the initiative they have been building since Day 1
morning, in front of a room that now knows exactly where to push.

The point is not the pitch. It is that **eight leaders practise asking the six questions
out loud**, so that in a real meeting next month, one of them does.

## Setup

- Front of the room, standing. Slides optional and discouraged — the one-page case is
  enough, on the visualiser or read out.
- Challenge checklist, one per person.
- Scoring cards, one per person per team.
- A visible timer. Five minutes means five minutes.

## Timing

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Format, rules, hand out checklists and cards |
| 5–75 | **Pitches** — 5 min pitch + 5 min challenge, per team |
| 75–85 | Room debrief — the patterns |
| 85–90 | Hand back scoring cards, brief the 30/60/90 |

With six teams that's 60 minutes of pitching. With eight teams, cut challenge time to
4 minutes and start on time.

**Then, separately, the 30/60/90 plan** — 10 minutes, in the closing session.

---

## Rules

Put these up and keep them up:

```
1. FIVE MINUTES. The timer is not a suggestion.

2. THE COMMITTEE'S JOB IS NOT TO BE KIND.
   Ask what a real funding board would ask.

3. ATTACK THE PROPOSAL, NEVER THE PERSON.

4. "I DON'T KNOW" IS AN ACCEPTABLE ANSWER.
   It goes on their action list, not against their score.

5. EVERYONE ASKS AT LEAST ONE QUESTION ACROSS THE SESSION.
```

**Rule 4 matters most.** Without it teams bluff, and bluffing is precisely the behavior
the two days have been trying to train out of them. State it, and the first time someone
says "I don't know, and I'd need to find out before committing" — stop and praise it.

---

## The challenge checklist

*One per person. Also handed to teams before they pitch — this is a rehearsal, not an
ambush.*

```
─── ON THE USE CASE ───────────────────────────────────────────────
□ Whose job gets measurably better? Name them.
□ Is this a real problem, or an available technology?
□ What do you do today, and what does that cost?
□ Who asked for this?

─── ON FEASIBILITY ────────────────────────────────────────────────
□ Does the data exist, and can you actually get to it?
□ Have you spent two hours doing it by hand in a chat window?
□ Why this rung of the ladder and not the one above?
□ What did you assume that you haven't checked?

─── ON RISK ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────
□ What happens when it's wrong? Who catches it?
□ Does that person have the time, the competence, and the authority
  to say no?
□ Cost and reversibility — where does this sit on the grid?
□ What's the risk tier, and who approves?
□ What goes in the log?

─── ON THE ORGANIZATION ───────────────────────────────────────────
□ Who owns this in production? Whose objectives is it in?
□ Whose budget carries it in year two?
□ Who resists this, and what do you say to them?
□ Which foundation does this need that you scored RED yesterday?

─── ON THE DECISION ───────────────────────────────────────────────
□ How will you know in 90 days?
□ Do you have a baseline, or are you about to start without one?
□ What would make you stop?
```

**The sharpest question in the set** is the RED foundation one. It connects the ambition
directly to Exercise 5 and it almost always finds something. If the room doesn't ask it,
ask it yourself — every time.

## Scoring card

*One per person, per team. Optional but recommended — it sharpens attention and gives each
team a profile rather than an impression.*

```
TEAM: ...............................   Score 1–5

                    1 = weak                    5 = strong
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
REAL PROBLEM        Technology looking      Named pain,        [ ]
                    for a use               quantified

FEASIBLE            Hand-waving             Tried it, knows    [ ]
                                            the limits

HONEST COSTS        Licence fee only        Full iceberg,      [ ]
                                            review time in

RISK HANDLED        "A human reviews it"    Named reviewer,    [ ]
                                            real authority,
                                            logged

OWNED               Nobody named            Named owner,       [ ]
                                            named budget

DECIDABLE           "We'll see how          Date, criterion,   [ ]
                    it goes"                kill switch
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
THE ONE THING I'D FIX BEFORE THIS GOES TO A REAL BOARD:

.................................................................
```

Collect these and hand each team its set. The free-text line is worth more than the
numbers — six independent readings of the weakest point in your case.

## Facilitator: running it well

**Before the first pitch,** set the tone in one sentence:
> *"Everything you say in the next five minutes, someone will say to a real board within
> a quarter. This is the cheap version."*

**Keep time visibly and cut at five minutes**, mid-sentence if necessary. The first cut is
uncomfortable and every subsequent pitch is better for it.

**Manage the challenge round.** Two failure modes:
- **Politeness.** If the first challenge round is soft, intervene: ask the RED-foundation
  question yourself, hard, and the room recalibrates.
- **Pile-on.** If one team is getting hammered, take the last question yourself and make it
  a constructive one. Nobody learns from being buried.

**Track the pattern across pitches.** You are collecting the debrief as you go. Note:
- which questions nobody could answer
- which team improved on the previous team's weakness (this happens visibly by pitch three)
- who said "I don't know" well

**The team whose case failed the arithmetic.** Make sure they present. Introduce them
deliberately: *"this team found their case doesn't pay. Listen to what they did next."*
It reframes the whole session away from advocacy and toward judgment.

---

## Room debrief — 10 minutes

Name the patterns. These recur:

- **The strongest pitches were the narrowest.** Almost always true. Ask the room whether
  they noticed.
- **Almost every team underestimated review time.** Show of hands: who revised it upward
  during Exercise 8?
- **Ownership was the hardest question.** Usually the one with the longest silences.
- **Data access came up in nearly every challenge.** Point back to Exercise 5 — it was the
  most common red foundation, and here it is again, arriving as a constraint on every
  proposal in the room.
- **"What would make us stop" got better with every pitch.** By pitch four the answers are
  specific. Name that improvement — it's the room learning in real time.

Then:

> *"Eight proposals, and the questions that broke them were the same six every time.
> That's the useful discovery. You don't need a framework to evaluate an AI proposal —
> you need six questions and the willingness to ask them when nobody else in the room
> will."*

## The 30/60/90 plan

*10 minutes, individual, in the closing session. Paper only.*

```
NEXT 30 DAYS
One thing I will personally do with AI, weekly:
....................................................................

One conversation I will have, and with whom:
....................................................................

One thing I will find out:
(data access? shadow usage? what it costs us today? the correction rate?)
....................................................................

NEXT 60 DAYS
One use case moving, with a named owner:
....................................................................

One governance thing started:
(the one-page rule? the inventory?)
....................................................................

NEXT 90 DAYS
One pilot running against a real baseline, decision date on the calendar:
....................................................................

Decision date: ...........  Who decides: ...........................
```

**Then pair up.** Each person tells one other person their 30-day commitment and exchanges
contact details, with an agreement to check in at thirty days.

That single step measurably raises follow-through, costs three minutes, and is the last
thing they do before the close.

## Variations

- **Short on time (45 min):** three teams pitch, chosen by a quick room vote on the
  Exercise 4 quick wins. The rest submit their one-pager for written peer feedback. Less
  good, still works.
- **Large room (10+ teams):** run two parallel committees in separate corners, then have
  the winner of each pitch to the whole room. Adds competitive energy and halves the time.
- **Senior-only room:** have participants pitch **each other's** cases — you present the
  case from the team next to you, from their one-pager alone. Brutal test of whether the
  document stands up without its author, which is exactly what happens when a paper goes
  to a board.

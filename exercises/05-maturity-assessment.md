# Exercise 5 — Honest Maturity Assessment

**Module 5** · Individual, then table discussion · 30 minutes + 10 minutes debrief

---

## Purpose

Two things, and the second matters more.

**First:** participants locate their organization on the stage model with evidence rather
than aspiration. Most place themselves a stage and a half too high, then move down when
asked for evidence.

**Second, and this is the real exercise:** people from the *same organization* disagree
about where it stands. That disagreement is a finding. It usually means the leadership
team has never had this conversation, and it explains a great deal about why the AI effort
is going the way it is.

## Setup

- Worksheet, one per **person**. This starts individual and stays individual until Part 4.
- Tables of 4–6, ideally mixing people from different parts of the organization.
- Participants need their Exercise 4 sheet in front of them for Part 5.

> **Say this before starting:** *"Nobody is scoring your organization but you, and nothing
> leaves this room. The only wrong answer is a flattering one."*

## Timing

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Instructions |
| 2–7 | **Part 1** — place yourself on the stages |
| 7–12 | **Part 2** — the two hard questions |
| 12–20 | **Part 3** — readiness checklist, RAG-scored |
| 20–28 | **Part 4** — compare at your table |
| 28–30 | **Part 5** — the collision check |
| 30–40 | Debrief |

## Part 1 — Where are you? (5 min)

> Circle one. Then write the **evidence** — what is actually true, not what is planned.

```
[ ] 1 · EXPERIMENTING    Individuals trying tools, largely invisible to leadership
[ ] 2 · PILOTING         Sanctioned projects with defined scope
[ ] 3 · OPERATIONALIZING Something running for real, users depend on it
[ ] 4 · SCALING          Repeatable across multiple areas, second projects are cheaper
[ ] 5 · TRANSFORMING     Processes redesigned around what is now possible

EVIDENCE FOR MY ANSWER:
....................................................................
....................................................................
```

---

## Part 2 — The two hard questions (5 min)

> Answer these before you look at your Part 1 answer again.

```
Q1. Name something running in production today that a real user depends on.

    ................................................................

    If the answer is "nothing", you are at stage 1 or 2 — regardless of
    how much has been spent or how many pilots have run.


Q2. If your most AI-enthusiastic person left tomorrow, what would stop?

    ................................................................

    If the answer is "everything", you have a person, not a capability.


DO I WANT TO CHANGE MY STAGE?    [ ] No    [ ] Yes → new stage: ......
```

**Facilitator:** circulate during this. The moment someone visibly pauses at Q1 is the
moment the exercise is working. Say nothing.

---

## Part 3 — Readiness checklist (8 min)

> Score each **green / amber / red**. Red is not a failure — it is your roadmap.

| Foundation | The question that decides it | G | A | R |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Data** | Does the material exist, is it current, is it consistent? | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| **Access** | Can we technically reach it, and are we permitted to? | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| **Skills** | Is there someone who can configure, operate, and evaluate this? | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| **Governance** | Is there a policy people can follow and an approval path? | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| **Sponsorship** | Is there a leader whose own objectives depend on this working? | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| **Budget** | Is year two funded, including review time? | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| **Measurement** | Do we have a baseline — taken before we started? | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |

**Scoring rules, and enforce them:**

- **If you don't know, it's red.** Not amber. Not knowing *is* the condition.
- **Sponsorship is green only if you can name the person.** "Leadership supports it" is
  amber at best.
- **Measurement is green only if a baseline already exists.** "We'll measure it" is red.

```
MY THREE REDS:  1. ..................................................
                2. ..................................................
                3. ..................................................
```

---

## Part 4 — Compare at your table (8 min)

> Go round the table. Each person says their stage and their three reds.
>
> **Where two people from the same organization disagree, stop and work out why.**

Three questions to work through:

```
1. What was the widest disagreement about the stage?
   ..............................................................

2. Which foundation came up red most often at this table?
   ..............................................................

3. Does anyone at this table know something the others didn't?
   (Shadow usage, a pilot nobody mentioned, a data source, a
    governance decision already taken)
   ..............................................................
```

**Facilitator note:** question 3 produces the best material in the exercise. In almost
every delivery, somebody discovers a pilot, a tool licence, or a policy their colleagues
did not know existed. That is a live finding about their organization's internal
visibility, and it is worth naming in the debrief.

---

## Part 5 — The collision check (2 min)

> Get out your Exercise 4 sheet from yesterday.

```
MY STRATEGIC BET:  ..................................................

FOUNDATIONS IT NEEDS:  ..............................................

OF THOSE, HOW MANY DID I SCORE RED?  ................................
```

> **If your strategic bet depends on a foundation you scored red, you have found the
> actual first project.** It is probably not the one you were planning to pitch.

---

## Debrief — 10 minutes

Ask in this order:

**1. "Hands up — who moved themselves down a stage after the two questions?"**
Usually a third to a half of the room. Let people see that.

**2. "Which foundation was red most often?"**
Take a show of hands per foundation. **Data and measurement win almost every time.**
Write the two winners on the board — they are the organization's real starting point, and
they are more useful than any use case discussed yesterday.

**3. "Where did people from the same organization disagree most?"**
Draw this out properly. The common ones:
- One person counts individual tool use as stage 1; another counts it as nothing at all
- Someone counts a pilot as "in production" because it has users; someone else doesn't
- Sponsorship: one person names an executive, another says that person has never mentioned it

**4. "Did anyone learn something about their own organization in the last eight minutes?"**
This is where the shadow pilots surface.

**5. The collision.** *"Whose strategic bet needs a foundation they scored red?"*
Usually most of the room. Land it:

> *"That is not a reason to abandon the bet. It is the reason the bet keeps not happening.
> The three reds are your next six months — and the honest version of your roadmap starts
> with them, not with the use case."*

---

## What they take away

```
MY STAGE:                     ......................................
MY EVIDENCE:                  ......................................
MY THREE RED FOUNDATIONS:     ......................................
THE FIRST ONE I WILL FIX:     ......................................
WHO ELSE NEEDS TO SEE THIS:   ......................................
```

That last line matters. This assessment is worth more shared with two or three colleagues
than filed. Suggest they take a photograph of it before they leave.

---

## Variations

- **Whole-organization cohort:** collect the stage placements anonymously and show the
  distribution on screen. A leadership team seeing its own spread — stage 1 to stage 4
  across eight people — is a more powerful intervention than anything you can say.
- **Short on time (15 min):** Parts 2 and 3 only. The two questions and the checklist carry
  the exercise; Part 1 is warm-up.
- **Mixed-organization public course:** skip Part 4's same-organization comparison and
  instead have people compare *across* organizations. Different lesson, still useful —
  everyone discovers they are not behind.

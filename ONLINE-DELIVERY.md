# Online Delivery Guide — Solo, No Breakouts

**For:** the 9/22–9/23 online delivery · one instructor · one organization · everyone on a
ChatGPT Enterprise seat.

The course was written for an in-person room with teams, cards, sticky notes, and A3
worksheets. **None of that is used online.** This guide is the authoritative run for each
exercise on a video call with no breakout rooms. Where it differs from an exercise's own
facilitator guide, **this guide wins for the 9/22–9/23 delivery.**

## The two moves that replace every team

1. **Individual on your own seat** — each person does the work themselves, shares highlights
   in chat. This is now the default, and it fits the Enterprise setup: everyone drives, no
   one spectates.
2. **Whole-room facilitated** — you run it live on your shared screen; the room contributes
   by voice and chat. Used where the *interaction* is the lesson (role-play, tabletop).

**Breakout rooms: none.** If you ever want one, the exercises' original team versions still
apply — but nothing here needs one.

## Set up before you start (put in the invite / first slide)

- A **shared worksheet doc** (Google Doc / OneDrive / a shared OneNote) with a copy of each
  worksheet — participants type into their own copy or a personal tab. Replaces every
  printout.
- A **shared folder or chat pin** with the MTS documents (Ex2 pack A–D; Ex3 source packet +
  outputs; the 8b source packet as an uploadable PDF **and** txt/docx).
- Decide your voting mechanism: the meeting platform **poll**, or a quick **chat "type M / V
  / W"**. You'll use it repeatedly.
- Ask people to keep cameras on for the discussion beats — online debriefs die without faces.

## Exercise-by-exercise

### Ex1 — Jargon Decoder · whole-room · ~15 min
Put the claims on screen one at a time (one per slide, or a shared list). For each: room
votes **Meaningful / Vague / Wrong** in the poll or chat. Read the split out loud, call on
two people to justify opposite votes, then reveal. Finish: each person types **one rewritten
claim** into the chat. No cards, no piles.
*Watch:* keep it fast — 60–90 seconds per claim. The energy is in the disagreement, not the sorting.

### Ex2 — Prompting Workshop · individual · 60 + 10
Already individual and online-native — run it as written. Documents come from the shared
folder (upload, don't paste). For the head-to-head at minute 46, put two volunteers' outputs
on your screen side by side instead of "turn to a neighbour." Use the shared Custom GPT
option if you built one — online, it guarantees everyone queries the same grounded source.

### Ex3 — Spot the Failure · individual → whole-room reveal · 30 + 15
Post the source packet and the four outputs to the shared folder. Everyone hunts defects
**individually**, typing each finding into their own worksheet tab (or DM to you) — private,
so nobody copies. At time, ask "**how many did you find?**" in a poll: watch the numbers
cluster below 12. Then reveal the twelve on your screen, one at a time, checking each against
the source live. The confidence→reveal→quiet arc works even better online because the count
is public and anonymous. **Keep the seeded outputs — do not have them generate live.**

### Ex4 — Use-Case Portfolio · individual · 60 + 15
Each person builds **their own** portfolio for their own area, in their worksheet tab: 12
candidates (Part 1), scored on the grid (Part 2), pick one quick win + one strategic bet
(Part 3). Because it's one organisation, you can, if you like, spend five minutes at the end
pooling everyone's quick wins on a shared screen and running a **poll vote** for the room's
favourite — that keeps the "portfolio" feel without teams.
**Downstream change:** each person now carries *their own* quick win into Ex6, 7, 8, and the
capstone — not a team's. That's arguably better for a leadership course; everyone leaves with
their own initiative.
*Enterprise blind-spot check still applies — only after minute 12.*

### Ex5 — Maturity Assessment · individual → whole-room · 30 + 10
Run the individual scoring as written. For the debrief, use a **poll**: "Which stage is our
organisation at?" The spread on screen *is* the exercise — one org, one call, visible
disagreement. Draw out why finance and engineering scored it differently.

### Ex6 — Resistance Role-Play · whole-room fishbowl · ~30 min
This is the exercise that can't go individual, and online with no breakouts you run it as a
**fishbowl** on the main call:
- **You play the leader** carrying an initiative (use a volunteer's Ex4 quick win). Invite
  three volunteers to voice the objector roles — skeptical senior engineer, anxious analyst,
  compliance officer — reading from the role cards you paste to them by DM.
- Run one conversation live, 6–8 minutes. The room watches. Then debrief in chat: *what
  worked, what made it worse?*
- Run a second round with fresh volunteers and a different quick win if time allows.
- Everyone who isn't speaking has a job: type in chat the moment the leader **lost** the room.
*Loses universal rehearsal; keeps the learning visible to all. The compliance officer's
unanswerable questions still set up Module 7.*

### Ex7 — Incident Tabletop · whole-room · 35 working + 12 debrief
Run the whole room as one incident team on the main call. Reveal **inject 1** on your screen;
the room works the response by voice/chat for ~10 min while you capture decisions on a shared
screen. Do **not** reveal inject 2 until 1 is worked. Repeat for injects 2 and 3. The
designed discovery — *"we would not have detected this ourselves"* — lands as a room. Keep a
hard timer per inject; online discussion sprawls.

### Ex8 — Business Case · individual · 45
Each person builds the one-pager for **their own** Ex4 quick win in their worksheet tab: the
ladder (step 1), the arithmetic (step 2), the iceberg, the verdict. Share two or three on
screen and challenge them live. The rung-2 callback to 8b and the Enterprise cost
pressure-test both apply per person.

### Ex8b — Build Your Own Grounded Assistant · individual · 35
Online-native — everyone builds on their own seat. Share the MTS packet as an uploadable
file in chat. **Pre-flight the Custom GPT permission today.** Have finishers share their
assistant's link in chat so others can try it (that *is* step 7, sharing). Protect steps 5–6
(break it, fix it). If Custom GPTs are locked, use the single-chat-upload fallback in the
lab guide.

### Capstone — Pitch & Challenge · whole-room, volunteer pitches · ~60 min
Every-team pitching doesn't exist online-solo. Instead: **4–6 volunteers pitch** their own
initiative, 3–4 minutes each, screen-sharing their one-pager. The rest of the room is the
investment committee, challenging by voice and chat using the checklist. Everyone scores each
pitch in a **poll**. Close with the room patterns and the 30/60/90 plan. This fits online
timing far better than a full team-by-team round and keeps the point: leaders practising the
six questions out loud.

## What this does to the schedule (good news)

Removing team formation and breakout setup/teardown (roughly 2–3 min each, seven times)
reclaims **~15–25 min per day** and makes several exercises tighter. Combined with the
run-sheet cut plan, **the Day 2 overrun is largely absorbed** — the whole-room capstone alone
saves ~15–30 min versus team-by-team. Re-check against the run sheet, but online-solo is the
*easier* delivery to land on time, not the harder one.

## Online facilitation notes (solo)

- **You can't circulate.** Replace "walk the room" with the chat: ask people to post findings,
  counts, and their "I CHECK" lines there, so you can see who's stuck without breakout rooms.
- **Silence is normal online.** Call on people by name; a cold "what did you find, [name]?"
  beats "anyone?"
- **Protect debriefs.** Same rule as in-person: cut exercise minutes, never debrief minutes.
- **Have the shared doc and MTS files ready to paste-link before each exercise** — dead air
  while you hunt for a link is worse online than in a room.

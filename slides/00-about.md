## Two Days, One Question

> **"So what are we doing about AI?"**

Someone is going to ask you that. Probably soon. Possibly this week.

By Wednesday evening you will have an answer you can defend — with a use case, a
number, a risk register, and a name next to it.

Notes:

Open with this. Ask the room who has already been asked the question, and by whom.
It surfaces the real pressure people are under and sets the tone that this is a
decision-making course, not a technology tour.

---

## Pre-requisites and Expectations

* **No technical background is assumed**
  - No programming, no math, no prior AI exposure
  - If you can use a web browser, you can do every exercise in this course

* **Bring a real problem**
  - A process, a bottleneck, a pile of documents from your own area
  - You will work on it for two days and leave with a plan for it

* **Curiosity, and skepticism**
  - Ask a lot of questions
  - Argue with me — the claims in this course should survive being challenged

Notes:

## Our Teaching Philosophy

* Emphasis on **judgment**, not tools
  - Tools change every six months; the questions don't

* **Highly interactive** — questions and disagreement are welcome at any moment

* **Learn by doing** — every module ends with you applying it to your own organization

* **Honest about limits** — you will see this technology fail, on purpose, in front of you

Notes:

Stress that we deliberately show failures. Leaders who have only seen polished demos
make bad decisions.

## What You Will Walk Out With

Not notes. **Artifacts.**

| Day 1 | Day 2 |
|---|---|
| A personal prompt library | An honest maturity assessment |

| A scored use-case portfolio | Three governance controls |
| | A one-page business case |
| | A 30/60/90 day plan |

Notes:

Show this early. It tells people the course produces something and makes the exercises
feel like work, not filler.

## The Shape of the Two Days

**Day 1 — Understanding AI and finding the value**

1. What AI actually is (and isn't)
2. Working with AI: prompting as a leadership skill
3. Capabilities, limits, and trust
4. Finding and prioritizing business value

**Day 2 — Leading the organization through AI**

5. AI strategy and the stages of AI success
6. Building an AI-ready culture
7. Responsible AI, risk, and governance
8. Planning, investing, and no-code enablement — then the pitch

## Ground Rules

* **Ask the question when you have it.** Not at the end.

* **The parking lot is real.** Anything organization-specific goes on the board and gets
  answered before we close.

* **No stupid questions, and no stupid answers.** If I don't know, I will say so.

* **Nothing sensitive goes into a public AI tool during this class.** We will talk about
  exactly why in Module 7.

Notes:

That last rule matters. Say it on day one and enforce it — participants will be tempted
to paste real internal documents into the exercises.
